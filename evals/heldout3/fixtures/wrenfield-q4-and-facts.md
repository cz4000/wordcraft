Brackenbridge Repair Café
- started May 2022, monthly, 2nd Saturday, 10–1, St Aidan's church hall
- 14 volunteers (9 "fixers": electrical x3, sewing x2, bikes x2, general/furniture x2), the rest on welcome desk/tea
- 2025: 611 items brought in, 412 fixed (67%). 2024: 498 items, 301 fixed (60%)
- top things: lamps, toasters, kettles, trousers/hems, bikes (punctures, brakes), vacuums, kids' toys
- feedback forms: 87% said they would have thrown the item away otherwise (about 230 forms back last year, so not everyone fills it in)
- weight: Dee uses the online calculator thing — about 1.9 tonnes kept out of landfill in 2025. not sure how accurate
- waiting times: often 45 min+ by 11am, we stop taking items at about 12:15. ~20-25 people a session go home without anything looked at (estimate)
- electrical: only 3 fixers can do electrical and only 1 (Ken, 74) can PAT test, using his own tester which is old

What the money's for (asking £4,800):
- new PAT tester £650
- tool kits / consumables £900
- training: 3 new volunteers on the electrical safety course at Brackenbridge College, £420 each = £1,260
- room hire for a 2nd monthly session — Hollins Library community room, Weds evening 6–8:30, £40/session x 12 = £480 (library says they'd waive it if we partner on their "Fix It Week" so maybe not needed??)
- publicity, flyers, translation into Polish + Urdu: £510
- volunteer expenses + tea/biscuits: £1,000 for the year

- Hollins estate: lots of older residents + families on low incomes, hardly anyone from there comes to St Aidan's (other side of the ring road, 2 buses). The library is on the estate.
- Mrs Okafor from the residents' association said people "would come if it was on the doorstep"
- also want to start a simple "learn to fix it" thing — fixers show you how, so you can do it yourself next time. one of our sewing volunteers (Halina) already does this informally
- what we could measure: items in/fixed, repair rate, how many from Hollins postcodes (we already take the first half of the postcode on the sign-in sheet), feedback form "would you have thrown it away", how many people learned to do it themselves (new question on the form?), turned-away numbers
