## D. Outside the property

**D1 Chimney stacks — Condition 3.** There are two chimney stacks. The front stack is shared with No. 12 and appears in reasonable condition, with some weathering to the brickwork. The rear stack is in poor condition. Mortar joints are open and eroded on all visible faces, the cement flaunching around the pots is cracked, and the stack appears to lean slightly toward the rear garden. A television aerial bracket is fixed to the rear stack and is adding to the load on it. You should have the rear stack inspected by a competent builder with scaffold access, who will advise whether repointing and new flaunching is enough or whether the stack needs partial rebuilding above roof level. We would expect costs in the region of £1,800 to £3,500 depending on the extent of work and scaffolding requirements. The aerial should be relocated.

**D2 Roof coverings — Condition 2.** The main roof is covered in natural slate. Approximately six slates are slipped or missing on the rear slope, and there is moss growth on the lower courses. The rear addition roof is covered in concrete interlocking tiles, which appear in fair condition for their age. A roofer should replace the missing slates and check the remaining fixings. Some slates may be suffering from "nail sickness" (corroded fixings), common at this age; the roof will eventually need re-covering, but not in the short term.

**D3 Rainwater pipes and gutters — Condition 2.** Front gutters and downpipes are cast iron and appear serviceable, although they need painting. Rear gutters are uPVC. A joint in the rear gutter above the kitchen door is leaking; there was staining to the wall below following the overnight rain. The downpipe at the rear left corner discharges onto the patio rather than into a gully, and water is likely to be splashing against the wall here (see E3).

**D4 Main walls — Condition 1.** The walls are solid brick, laid in Flemish bond, with a painted render finish to the ground-floor front bay. A line of drilled holes along the base of the rear wall indicates that a chemical damp-proof course has been injected at some point; we do not know when. The rear patio has been raised so that its surface is within about 75mm of the internal floor level, which means it is above the damp-proof course and is "bridging" it (see E3).

**D5 Windows — Condition 2.** Front windows are uPVC double-glazed units, probably installed around 2008. The rear bedroom retains its original timber sliding sash window, which has wet rot in the lower sill and bottom rail. The rot to the rear sash should be repaired by a joiner before it spreads; repair rather than replacement may be possible.

**D6 Outside doors — Condition 1.** The front door is a recent composite door in good condition. The rear kitchen door is timber, half-glazed, and in fair condition.

**D8 Other joinery and finishes — Condition 1.** Paint to fascias and window surrounds is flaking; redecorate within a couple of years.

**D9 Other — Condition 2.** The low brick front boundary wall leans slightly toward the pavement and has some loose coping bricks. Ownership of the wall should be confirmed (see H).

## E. Inside the property

**E1 Roof space — Condition 2.** Accessed via a hatch on the landing; there is no ladder or lighting. The roof timbers are original and appear generally sound. There are old flight holes from wood-boring insects in one purlin, but no fresh dust (frass) was seen and the activity appears historic. Insulation is approximately 100mm of mineral wool; current recommendations are around 270mm. A disused galvanised water tank could be removed.

**E2 Ceilings — Condition 1.** Original lath and plaster in the front rooms and hall, with decorative cornicing in the front reception. A hairline crack across the front bedroom ceiling is typical and not of concern.

**E3 Walls and partitions — Condition 3.**

*Damp, rear reception.* Damp meter readings were high along the rear wall of the rear reception, to a height of about 600mm, and along part of the side wall where it meets the rear wall. There is salt staining and the plaster sounds hollow ("blown") when tapped. In our opinion the most likely causes are the raised patio bridging the damp-proof course (D4) and water splashing from the rear downpipe (D3). We recommend that the patio is lowered so that it is at least 150mm below the internal floor level, that the downpipe is connected to a gully, and that the wall is then allowed to dry out before re-plastering with a breathable lime-based plaster. Further chemical injection is unlikely to solve the problem while the patio remains at its current level, and you should be cautious of any contractor who recommends this as the only remedy.

*Chimney breast, rear bedroom.* The chimney breast in the rear bedroom has been removed at first-floor level. The chimney breast above this level, in the roof space, and the stack above the roof (D1) remain in place. We could not see how the remaining masonry is supported; the support may be concealed within the ceiling, or it may be absent. If it is absent or inadequate, this is a safety risk. You should ask your legal adviser to find out whether building regulations approval was obtained for this work and whether a structural engineer's design exists. If no evidence is available, a structural engineer should inspect before you exchange contracts.

**E4 Floors — Condition 2.** The ground floor is suspended timber. There is some springiness near the kitchen doorway, which may indicate a weakened joist or loose boards. The air bricks at the front of the property, which ventilate the space beneath the floor, are partly blocked by soil and a planter. They should be cleared to reduce the risk of timber decay. First-floor floors were firm underfoot.

**E5 Fireplaces, chimney breasts and flues — Condition 1.** The front reception has an open Victorian fireplace with a tiled surround and cast-iron insert. We did not test it. Have the flue swept and checked before use. The rear reception fireplace has been blocked up without a ventilator; one should be fitted.

**E6 Built-in fittings — Condition 1.** Kitchen units, probably fitted around 2015, are in fair condition. The worktop is swollen beside the sink.

**E7 Woodwork — Condition 1.** The original staircase and balustrade are firm. Some gaps between balusters exceed the modern standard, which may matter if you have young children.

**E8 Bathroom fittings — Condition 2.** Modern bath with shower over, basin and WC. The silicone seals around the bath are perished and should be replaced to prevent water getting into the floor. The extractor fan did not work when switched on.

## F. Services

**F1 Electricity — Condition 3.** The consumer unit, under the stairs, is an older type with rewireable fuses and no residual current device (RCD) protection. Most visible wiring is in modern PVC cable, but an older cable was seen in the roof space. No recent electrical safety certificate (EICR) was provided. We recommend that a registered electrician inspects and tests the installation before you exchange contracts. It is likely that, at a minimum, the consumer unit will need replacing, and partial rewiring may be needed.

**F2 Gas and oil — Condition 1.** The gas meter is in a cupboard in the front reception alcove. The vendor states the boiler has been serviced annually.

**F3 Water — Condition 1.** The internal stopcock is under the kitchen sink. No lead pipework was seen.

**F4 Heating — Condition 2.** Heating is by a gas-fired combination boiler in the kitchen, with radiators throughout. A label indicates installation in 2012. The vendor mentioned that the system pressure "drops sometimes" and needs topping up, which may indicate a small leak in the system or a fault in the boiler. You should ask for the service records and the gas safety certificate. Boilers of this age are approaching the end of their typical working life and you should budget for replacement in the next few years.

**F6 Drainage — Not inspected.** The inspection chamber cover in the rear garden has been painted and sealed and could not be lifted. Drains of this age are usually clay and may have cracked or displaced joints. Given the age of the drains and the presence of a mature tree nearby (see G), you may wish to commission a CCTV drainage survey (typically £150–£250).

## G. Grounds

The rear garden is approximately 11 metres long, laid to lawn beyond the raised patio, and enclosed by timber fencing in fair condition. A timber shed at the end of the garden has a rotten base and is near the end of its life. A mature sycamore tree stands in the garden of No. 16, approximately 6 metres from the rear addition. We saw no evidence that the tree is affecting the building, but its roots may affect the drains. The rear garden gate opens onto a shared passageway running behind the terrace.

- Any guarantee for the chemical damp-proof course (D4), and whether it is transferable. Note that a guarantee may be invalidated by the raised patio.

## I. Risks

**Risks to the building:** Water penetration and possible instability of the rear chimney stack (D1); damp in the rear reception (E3); possible lack of support to the remaining chimney breast (E3).

**Risks to the grounds:** Possible root activity from the neighbouring sycamore affecting drainage (F6, G).

**Risks to people:** Outdated electrical installation without RCD protection (F1); possible unsupported masonry (E3). Only one smoke alarm was seen, a battery-powered unit in the hall; we recommend mains-wired, interlinked alarms on each floor and a carbon monoxide alarm near the boiler.

**Other risks or hazards:** The property is not in an area identified as at risk from radon. National flood mapping indicates a very low risk of flooding from rivers and surface water.
