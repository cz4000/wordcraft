stuff for grandad's thing - copied from the family group + texts

DATES ETC
- Desmond Arthur Hartnell, born 3 March 1941 in Kellsby, died Tues 20th Oct, 85
- married Nan (Patricia) 1963 - mum says 63, Auntie Carol says 64?? check
- Nan died 2019, he was on his own after that but wouldn't move in with anyone, "I've got the beans to think about"
- worked at the bus depot on Fenner Road 38 years, mechanic then workshop foreman. retired 1999? or 2001? Steve says it was the year of the big snow
- 3 kids - mum (Lynne), Carol, Uncle Steve. 6 grandkids, 2 great grandkids (Ava and baby Rory, he got to meet Rory in August which mum keeps saying she's so glad about)

FROM MUM
"He used to say buses were like people, they all make a noise when something's wrong, you just have to listen. He'd hear one go past the house and say 'that one's got a bad bearing.'"
He walked me to school every single day even though the depot was the other way and it made him late. Never told Nan.
He kept every birthday card any of us ever gave him in a shoebox. We found it last week in the wardrobe. Even the ones we made at school with the glitter coming off.

FROM CAROL
the allotment!! plot 14 at Holloway Lane for about 40 yrs. runner beans, he was obsessed. entered the show every year, won best runner beans once in about 1988 and never stopped talking about it. the rosette is still on the shed door
the marmalade - made it every January, Seville oranges, the whole house stank for a week. gave jars to everyone, the postman, the doctor's receptionist, the lady in the chemist
don't mention the stuff after Nan died, the drinking. we don't need that at the funeral, people know.
he had a terrible joke for everything. "why did the bus stop? because it saw the zebra crossing". he told that one at my wedding. TWICE

FROM STEVE (voice note, I typed it up)
taught me to drive in the depot yard on a Sunday when nobody was there. in a bus. well not a bus, I was 15, it was the works van. but he always told it as a bus and I stopped correcting him
he wasn't one for saying I love you, he'd check your tyres instead. every time you visited he'd go out and check your tyre pressure before you left. every time. even when I had a brand new car
he was in the depot brass band? no hang on that was his brother Ken. ignore that

FROM ME
- crosswords every morning, the cryptic one, in pen. he'd ring me "if he got stuck" which was never actually, he just wanted to tell me the answer
- when I failed my driving test the first time he said "the examiner didn't know what he was looking at"
- the last time I saw him (two weeks ago) he was going on about the beans, said he'd saved seed for next year and I should have it. it's in an envelope on the kitchen table with my name on
- he called everyone "chief". the vicar, the paperboy, me
