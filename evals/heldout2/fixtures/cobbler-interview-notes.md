Gerald Ombati — Corn St Shoe Repairs & Keys — talked Tues 20th, about 1hr in the shop (kept getting interrupted by customers, which was sort of the point)

age 71. born Kisumu, came over 1972 aged 18, "with one suitcase and a very bad coat"
- first job: Tannery in Halworth (gone now, flats). then apprenticed to old Mr Stanbury who had the shop on Corn St "he was a hard man but fair. he made me resole the same pair of boots eleven times before he let me touch a customer's shoe"
- took over shop 1981 when Stanbury retired. so 44 yrs his. (Stanbury had it since 1950s?? Gerald not sure. sign above door still says STANBURY underneath if you look, painted over)
- closing 20 Dec. Last day. Lease up, rent going from £9k to £16k a yr, landlord wants it for a "concept store"?? G laughed about this. not bitter he says. "I am 71. my back has been telling me to stop for ten years. now the landlord is telling me too. two against one"

the shop:
- tiny. smells of leather + glue + the paraffin heater. radio always on (cricket if on)
- wall of keys, ~3,000 blanks he reckons
- bell on door from Stanbury's day
- photos of customers' dogs behind the counter?? (asked - "people bring their dogs, I take a photo, I don't know why I started, now I can't stop". about 200 photos)

what he's fixed:
- "everything. shoes, bags, belts, dog leads, a saddle once, a ballerina's pointe shoes — she was crying, opening night"
- wedding shoes on the morning of weddings "at least once a month, always a heel, always 8 in the morning"
- he resoled the Mayor's shoes (which mayor? "the one with the moustache" — don't name)
- Hardest job: a pair of boots a man's father had worn walking home from the war. "the leather was like paper. I didn't charge him. how can you charge for that"

quotes (verbatim-ish):
- "a good shoe will outlive a bad marriage"  (he laughed, said put it in)
- "people say nobody mends things anymore. it's not true. young people come in all the time now. they have watched videos. they want to know how."
- "the street has changed. the butcher, gone. the wool shop, gone. the bank, gone. now it's coffee coffee phones coffee. but the people are the same people"
- re retirement: "I will go to Kisumu for three months in January. I have not been for a long winter since 1990. my sister is 76 and she says hurry up"
- "I will miss the talking. the shoes, not so much."

customers while I was there:
- woman w/ toddler, keys cut, G gave toddler a lolly from a jar (jar of lollies under counter for 40 yrs he says)
- teenager w/ trainers sole coming off, G showed him how he'd glue it, told him to come back Thurs
- older man, Mr Pell, "been coming since 1983", said "where am I supposed to go now" — G said "you will have to buy new shoes like everybody else, Colin" and they both laughed

other bits:
- married to Ruth (d. 2017). 2 kids, son in Leeds (accountant), daughter is a nurse at the General
- nobody taking over. he's selling the machines to a guy in Pendlebury who runs a repair van
- he's done a 2 for 1 on resoles for the last month "so I'm not stuck with anyone's shoes"
- ANY SHOES NOT COLLECTED BY 18 DEC go to the charity shop — he asked us to print that please!! he's got about 40 pairs on the shelf uncollected, some from 2019
