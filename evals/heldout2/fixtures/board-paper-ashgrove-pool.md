**Paper 7 of 11**
**Title:** Ashgrove Pool — options for the future of the facility
**Author:** Delphine Marsh, Chief Executive (with input from R. Okafor, Finance & Resources Manager, and the Facilities team)

### 1. Purpose of this paper

1.1 This paper asks the Board to decide between three options for Ashgrove Pool, following the condition survey received in September and ahead of the end of the current council funding agreement on 31 March.

1.2 It also asks the Board to note the outcome of the informal user survey held over the summer, and to approve the release of £18,500 from designated reserves to fund the next stage of whichever option is chosen.

1.3 Trustees will recall that Ashgrove was last discussed at the June meeting (Paper 4, minutes item 6), where it was agreed that the executive would bring options back "in the autumn".

### 2. Background

2.1 Ashgrove Pool opened in 1974 and was transferred to the Trust from Fenwick Valley District Council in 2011 under a 25-year lease, alongside Millbrook Leisure Centre and the Ashgrove Community Hall. The building comprises a 25m six-lane main pool, a learner pool, a changing village (refurbished 2009), a small café area (currently closed except at weekends), the plant room, and the first-floor viewing gallery, which has been used as storage since 2019.

2.2 Under the lease the Trust is responsible for internal repairs and maintenance. The council remains responsible for "structural and roof elements", but the definition of "structural" has been the subject of disagreement in the past (see the 2017 dispute over the gallery stairs). Officers have been asked whether the roof membrane and pool tank fall within the council's obligation; we do not yet have a formal answer.

2.3 Ashgrove currently receives an annual management grant of £96,000 from the council. The current funding agreement ends on 31 March. The council has indicated, informally, that any replacement agreement is likely to be at a lower level. Figures between £60,000 and £75,000 have been mentioned in conversation, but nothing is confirmed and the council's own budget will not be set until February.

2.4 The pool's operating deficit (before grant) for the last financial year was £141,200. After grant, the net cost to the Trust was £45,200, which was absorbed by surpluses at Millbrook (principally the gym). The forecast deficit for this year is slightly higher, at around £152,000 before grant, mainly because of energy costs, although the new pool cover installed in May has helped.

### 3. Usage

3.1 Usage at Ashgrove has recovered since the pandemic but remains below 2019 levels. Total visits last year were 61,340, against 74,900 in 2019 (down 18%). Millbrook pool, by comparison, is at 96% of its 2019 level.

3.2 The breakdown of visits is broadly as follows:

- Swimming lessons (Learn to Swim programme): 22,800 visits (37%)
- Schools (curriculum swimming, nine primary schools): 9,100 visits (15%)
- Public and lane swimming: 17,400 visits (28%)
- Clubs (Ashgrove Otters, Fenwick Masters, the sub-aqua club): 8,650 visits (14%)
- Other (aquafit, parties, GP rehab referrals): 3,390 visits (6%)

3.3 The Learn to Swim programme is at capacity, with a waiting list of approximately 340 children across both sites, of whom around 210 have Ashgrove as their first preference. Lessons are the only part of the Ashgrove programme that makes a surplus.

3.4 Seven of the nine schools that use Ashgrove have no realistic alternative within the 20-minute coach journey the schools say they can manage within a lesson slot. Millbrook's programme is full on weekday daytimes. This is relevant to the statutory requirement for primary school swimming, although that duty sits with the schools and the education authority rather than with the Trust.

3.5 The café made a small loss of £3,100 last year and is not material to the options below.

### 4. Condition of the building

4.1 The Trust commissioned a full condition survey from Harland Baird Surveyors, received in draft in September and in final form on 27 October. The key findings are:

(a) **Roof.** The flat-roof membrane over the main pool hall is at the end of its life, with two active leaks (one over the learner pool). Estimated replacement cost £310,000–£360,000, recommended within 12–18 months. The surveyor notes that it may be possible to patch the roof for a further two winters at around £15,000 a year, but "with increasing risk of water ingress to the roof void and corrosion to the steel structure".

(b) **Pool tank and filtration.** The main pool tank is sound. The filtration system (two sand filters dating from 1998) is working but inefficient. Replacement is estimated at £120,000 and would reduce chemical and energy costs by an estimated £14,000 a year.

(c) **Air handling.** The air handling unit dates from a 1996 upgrade and parts are no longer available. Failure would close the pool. Replacement with a heat-recovery unit is estimated at £240,000, with energy savings estimated at £22,000–£28,000 a year.

(d) **Changing village.** Serviceable; cosmetic works only (£25,000, not urgent).

(e) **Viewing gallery.** Asbestos-containing materials identified in the floor tiles (encapsulated, low risk if left undisturbed). Any works to the gallery would require removal, estimated at £18,000.

4.2 Total identified works over five years come to approximately £740,000–£790,000, of which around £670,000 (roof, filtration and air handling) is described as "essential to continued operation beyond two to three years".

4.3 A schedule of minor items (guttering, car park lighting, the ramp handrail), totalling roughly £34,000, is in the full report and is not included above.

### 5. The options

The executive has developed three options. A fourth, transferring the pool to a community organisation, was considered but discounted at this stage because no group has expressed interest and the liabilities would be difficult for a volunteer body to carry. It can be revisited.

**Option A — Close Ashgrove Pool at the end of the current funding agreement (31 March)**

5.1 The Trust would give notice to the council under clause 19 of the lease to surrender the pool element of the site (the hall would continue). Learn to Swim would be consolidated at Millbrook, with extended evening and weekend hours, but overall lesson capacity would fall by around 40%. Approximately 600 children currently in lessons would need to find places elsewhere or leave the programme.

5.2 Schools: the Trust would offer Millbrook slots where possible, but realistically no more than three of the nine schools could be accommodated.

5.3 Staffing: 14 posts are based at Ashgrove (9.6 FTE). The executive estimates that five or six staff could be redeployed to Millbrook. Redundancy costs for the remainder are estimated at £38,000–£52,000.

5.4 Financial effect: removes the net deficit (around £45,000–£56,000 a year depending on the level of grant) and avoids the capital works. One-off costs would include redundancy, decommissioning (£20,000–£30,000) and a possible dilapidations claim from the council under the lease (amount unknown; legal advice is being sought).

5.5 Reputational risk: high. The Ashgrove Otters have already written to all trustees (letter in the portal), and a petition with around 2,900 signatures was presented at the September council meeting.

**Option B — Continue operating with reduced hours and minimum essential repairs (the "hold" option)**

5.6 Public opening would be reduced to concentrate on lessons, schools and clubs: the pool would close on Mondays and from 1pm on Sundays. The roof would be patched for two winters, and the air handling and filtration works deferred.

5.7 This saves around £28,000 a year in staffing and utilities. The net deficit after a reduced grant (assumed at £65,000) would be roughly £60,000–£70,000 a year.

5.8 This option does not resolve the condition issues; it buys time, probably two to three years, to seek external funding or for the council to clarify its position on structural responsibilities. The risk of unplanned closure due to failure of the air handling unit is significant and there is no contingency for it. If the unit failed in winter, closure could be indefinite.

**Option C — Invest in the roof, air handling and filtration (around £670,000), funded by a mix of council capital, external grant and a loan**

5.9 The executive has had preliminary conversations with the council's capital team, who have said a contribution "in the region of" £250,000 might be possible from the council's leisure capital fund if the Trust can show matched funding and a ten-year business case. If the roof is accepted as the council's obligation, that figure could rise. An external bid to a national sports facilities fund (deadline 14 January) could realistically bring in £100,000–£150,000. The balance (£270,000–£320,000) would require borrowing. Indicative terms from the Trust's bank are seven years at about 7.1%, an annual repayment of approximately £52,000–£60,000.

5.10 Energy and chemical savings (£36,000–£42,000 a year combined) would offset most of the loan repayments. With the lessons programme expanded to clear part of the waiting list (an estimated £18,000 additional surplus), the business case shows Ashgrove reaching close to break-even after grant by year three, assuming a grant of £65,000.

5.11 Risks: Option C depends on all three funding sources coming through. Construction would close the pool for an estimated 16–20 weeks (most likely next summer). The Trust's reserves would fall below the policy minimum of three months' operating costs during the build unless the council contribution is paid in advance.

5.12 Borrowing above £100,000 requires approval by the Board by special resolution under Article 41, so a further paper would be needed in any case.

### 6. User survey

6.1 An informal survey ran from 3 July to 15 August, online and on paper at both sites. 1,214 responses were received. In summary:

- 88% used Ashgrove at least monthly
- 71% said they would not or could not travel to Millbrook if Ashgrove closed (main reasons: no car; bus route 14 does not run on Sundays; time)
- The most common comments concerned changing room temperature, parking, requests for more early-morning lane swimming, and café opening hours

6.2 The survey was not a formal consultation and should not be treated as statistically representative. If the Board chooses Option A, a formal consultation would be required, and the Trust's legal advice is that a minimum of eight weeks would be prudent. This would make a 31 March closure date very tight.

### 7. Staff

7.1 Staff are aware that a review is taking place but not of the options. The union representative has asked for early engagement. If an option involving redundancies is chosen, collective consultation obligations may apply depending on final numbers (legal advice is being confirmed).

7.2 The Ashgrove duty manager, Kemal Ashworth, has been with the Trust for 19 years and is due to retire next year regardless of the outcome.

### 8. Financial summary (indicative, five years, £000s)

| Option | Five-year effect compared with the current position |
|---|---|
| A — Close | Net saving of approx. 180–230 after one-off costs; excludes any dilapidations claim |
| B — Hold | Additional cost of approx. 300–350; excludes the cost of any unplanned closure |
| C — Invest | Additional cost of approx. 90–140 after funding and loan; assumes all three funding sources are secured |

These figures were prepared quickly and the Finance & Resources Committee has not yet reviewed them. The Chair of FRC has asked that this be minuted.

### 9. Equality and community impact

9.1 A full equality impact assessment has not yet been completed. Initial screening indicates that closure would fall disproportionately on older users (the aquafit and rehab referral programmes are 78% over-60s), on children from the three wards in the east of the district with the lowest car ownership, and on disabled swimmers: Ashgrove has the only pool hoist in the district apart from the hospital hydrotherapy pool, which is not open to the public.

### 10. Recommendation

10.1 The executive recommends that the Board:

(a) NOTES the condition survey findings and the user survey summary;
(b) AGREES in principle to pursue Option C, subject to a full business case and funding package being brought back to the Board in February;
(c) AGREES to submit a bid to the national facilities fund by 14 January;
(d) APPROVES the release of £18,500 from designated reserves to fund the business case, a structural engineer's report on the roof responsibility question, and bid-writing support;
(e) AGREES that, if the funding package has not been secured by 30 June, the executive will bring Option A or B back for decision; and
(f) AUTHORISES the Chief Executive to begin informal engagement with the council on the question of structural responsibilities.

10.2 Should the Board prefer Option A, the executive would ask for an additional £12,000 for legal advice and the formal consultation.

### 11. Other matters

11.1 The Communications Committee has asked that no decision is made public until staff have been briefed, which would be on the morning after the Board meeting.

11.2 For information, the Ashgrove Community Hall is unaffected by any of the options and its bookings are up 12% on last year.
