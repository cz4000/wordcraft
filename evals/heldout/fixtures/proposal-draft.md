# Proposal: Saturday Pop-Up Repair Clinics

**Prepared by:** Priya Natarajan, Community Programmes
**Draft 2 — for comments**

## The problem

Over the last year our branches have taken 1,140 calls and walk-ins from people asking whether we repair things: bikes, small appliances, clothing, phones. We don't, so we send them away. At the same time our customer survey in March found that 38% of members under 35 said they would visit more often "if there was something practical to do there". We think there's a gap we can fill cheaply.

## What we're proposing

A monthly Saturday "Repair Clinic" at three branches (Millbrook, Eastgate and Harlow Road), run with volunteer fixers. People bring a broken item and sit with a volunteer while it's repaired, so they learn something too. We'd provide tables, basic tools, tea and coffee, and promotion. The volunteers would provide the know-how.

Similar clinics run by other community groups report that about 60–70% of items brought in get fixed on the day. There is clear goodwill locally: when we posted a short notice on our noticeboard in August asking if anyone would volunteer, 23 people replied within two weeks, including two retired electricians and a seamstress.

Branch staff would open up, help set out the tables and look after the room on the day; the volunteers would do the repairs. We'd ask people to book a slot in advance so we can match items to the right volunteer, but we'd also take walk-ins where there's capacity. We'd like to accept electrical items as well as bikes and textiles, because small appliances were the most common thing people asked about.

## Pilot plan

- **November:** recruit and brief volunteers
- **December:** first clinics at all three branches
- **January:** review and decide whether to expand

## Budget

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Tool kits (3 branches) | £1,200 |
| Promotion (flyers, social posts) | £400 |
| Refreshments (3 months) | £450 |
| **Total** | **£2,050** |

## How we'll measure success

We'll know the pilot has worked if people enjoy it and it brings in a new audience. We'll ask attendees for feedback at the end of each session.

## Risks

The main risk is that volunteers drop out. We'll manage this by keeping a reserve list.
