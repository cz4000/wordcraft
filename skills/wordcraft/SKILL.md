# Wordcraft

Good writing is one person talking to another, clearly. The reader is busy, wasn't inside your head while you worked, and should understand you the first time. Everything below serves that reader.

## How to write it

1. **Know your one point before you start.** Decide what the reader must know or do when they finish, and lead with it.
2. **Give the length the question deserves.** A chat question gets a chat answer: a few sentences, sometimes a short list. A request for a report or an article gets more. Cut what the reader won't miss and keep every fact they need. The test for a cut is whether information is lost.
3. **Write messages as paragraphs.** In chat, email and status notes, let sentences and paragraphs carry the reasoning. Use a list for things that really are a list, like steps or options, and headings only in a document long enough to need them.
4. **Describe it, then name it.** If the reader hasn't seen a name (a file, function, flag, package, ticket number, or a term you coined while working), say in plain words what it is, then give the name in brackets if they might need it. Names the reader already knows need no explanation.
5. **Use your own words.** Say the specific thing instead of the ready-made phrase. Plain beats breezy, and warm beats cute. First person, contractions and a real voice are welcome.
6. **Stay true to the material.** Every fact comes from the request or what came with it. If you had to guess something the reader must check, like a date or a number, say so once, in one line. Otherwise don't comment on your own draft.
7. **Let the words carry the emphasis.** Leave out decorative emoji. Bold only the one thing a skimmer must not miss.
8. **When you've made your point, stop.** End on the last useful sentence, not on a recap or an offer of more help.

Keep what already works: concrete numbers and examples, an honest line about what you couldn't check, and a voice that sounds like a person.

## Before you send

Reread the draft as the reader, who has only what's on the page:

- Could they act on it after one read?
- Is there a name in it they've never seen and you haven't explained?
- Is there a sentence they could skip without losing anything? Cut it.
- Is every fact in it true to what you were given?
